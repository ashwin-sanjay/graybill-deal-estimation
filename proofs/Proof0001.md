# a branch-free eight-tent profile for every positive Gamma shape

## 1. Result and scope

Fix arbitrary real shapes

\[
 \alpha>0,\qquad \beta>0.
\]

This note constructs one bounded compactly supported correction profile for
the two-shape reduced Gamma problem.  The same eight-node template works for
all positive shapes, whether the shapes are equal, unequal, half-integral,
rational, or irrational.  Its parameters are explicit functions of the two
shapes followed by two finite selections: an integer finite-difference
resolution and a positive tent width.

The construction proves the following.

> **Unified eight-tent theorem.**  For every \(\alpha,\beta>0\), there are a
> finite sum \(q_{\alpha,\beta}\) of at most eight normalized product tents
> in \((0,1)\times(0,\infty)\) and a number
> \(\varepsilon_{\alpha,\beta}>0\) such that, simultaneously for every
> nuisance fraction \(0<e<1\),
> \[
>  2\varepsilon_{\alpha,\beta}T_e(q_{\alpha,\beta})
>   +\varepsilon_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}E_e(q_{\alpha,\beta})<0.
> \tag{1.1}
> \]
> Here \(T_e\) and \(E_e\) are the exact reduced linear trace and quadratic
> energy defined below.  Multiplication by the positive physical
> normalization therefore gives strict risk improvement throughout the
> interior nuisance interval.

The construction has three additional properties.

1. It is regular at \(\alpha=\beta\).  No coefficient contains
   \((\alpha-\beta)^{-1}\), and the same formula specializes exactly to the
   balanced six-jet formula.
2. It is covariant under exchanging the two shapes.  With the same canonical
   resolution and width,
   \[
    q_{\beta,\alpha}(1-L,F)=-q_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F).
   \tag{1.2}
   \]
3. It crosses every positive-shape moment wall because it is bounded and
   supported away from \(L=0\), \(L=1\), and \(F=\infty\).

There is no claim of a shape-independent positive margin on the whole open
quadrant.  As either shape tends to zero, the construction necessarily used
here moves toward a boundary face, its admissible tent width contracts, and
its coefficient or energy bounds can deteriorate.  “Uniform” in this note
means one finite formula and one finite classical selection for every shape,
not one numerical table or one lower bound valid on a noncompact shape
space.

For ordinary two-sample normal designs, the shapes are the corresponding
positive half residual degrees and (1.1) is a literal estimator-risk
improvement.  For non-half-integral positive shapes, the theorem concerns the
exact reduced Gamma experiment itself; it does not assert that an
irrational-shape experiment is generated by an integer sample size.

## 2. The reduced problem

Put

\[
 \nu=\alpha+\beta,
 \qquad p=\alpha\beta,
 \qquad c=\frac p\nu,
 \qquad r=\nu+\frac32.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus \(\nu,p,c>0\) and \(r>1\).  On

\[
 \Omega=(0,1)\times(0,\infty)
\]

define

\[
 \rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F)
 =L^{\beta-1}(1-L)^{\alpha-1}F^{1/2}
\tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
 N_e(L,F)
 =\alpha e(1-L)+\beta(1-e)L+\frac12F e(1-e).
\tag{2.3}
\]

For a bounded compactly supported Borel profile \(q\), let

\[
 T_e(q)=\iint_\Omega
   (L-e)q(L,F)\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F)N_e(L,F)^{-r}\,dF\,dL,
\tag{2.4}
\]

\[
 E_e(q)=\iint_\Omega
   q(L,F)^2\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F)N_e(L,F)^{-r}\,dF\,dL.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Both integrals are finite because the support of \(q\) is a compact subset
of \(\Omega\).  Also \(E_e(q)\ge0\).

The risk identity needed here is just the pointwise square expansion

\[
 (L-e+\varepsilon q)^2-(L-e)^2
 =2\varepsilon(L-e)q+\varepsilon^2q^2.
\tag{2.6}
\]

After integration, the reduced increment is

\[
 \Delta_e(\varepsilon,q)
 =2\varepsilon T_e(q)+\varepsilon^2E_e(q).
\tag{2.7}
\]

The full Gamma-source calculation differs from (2.7), apart from the positive
total mean-variance scale, by the common factor

\[
 P_{\alpha,\beta}(e)=
 \frac{\alpha^\alpha\beta^\beta\Gamma(r)}
 {2^{3/2}\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(3/2)}
 e^{\alpha+3/2}(1-e)^{\beta+3/2}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

It is strictly positive for \(\alpha,\beta>0\) and \(0<e<1\).  Hence the
corresponding invariant density is, explicitly,

\[
 \mathcal K_e(L,F)=P_{\alpha,\beta}(e)
 \rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F)N_e(L,F)^{-r}.
\]

Equivalently, the invariant Gamma risk functional, up to an additive oracle
term independent of the coefficient \(w\), is

\[
 \mathcal R_e(w)=
 \Sigma\iint_\Omega (w(L,F)-e)^2\mathcal K_e(L,F)\,dF\,dL,
 \qquad \Sigma>0.
\tag{2.9}
\]

For \(w=L+\varepsilon q\), subtracting the value at \(w=L\) in (2.9)
gives exactly

\[
 \mathcal R_e(L+\varepsilon q)-\mathcal R_e(L)
 =\Sigma P_{\alpha,\beta}(e)
 \{2\varepsilon T_e(q)+\varepsilon^2E_e(q)\}.
\tag{2.10}
\]

In the ordinary normal model, the same formula follows after separating the
centered oracle error from the contrast carrier; their cross term is zero.
For arbitrary non-half-integral shapes, (2.9) is the precise invariant Gamma
risk authority claimed here.  Thus no unstated physical sample-size
interpretation is needed.

The sign problem is therefore exactly the problem of finding one \(q\) for
which

\[
 \sup_{0\le e\le1}T_e(q)<0,
 \qquad
 \sup_{0\le e\le1}E_e(q)<\infty.
\tag{2.11}
\]

The closed interval is used in (2.11) only to obtain a uniform estimate.  The
strict physical statement uses its interior.

## 3. Centering the carrier

Let

\[
 \ell_* =\frac\alpha\nu,
 \qquad \Delta=\alpha-\beta,
 \qquad d_e=\beta-\nu e,
 \qquad h_e=\frac{e(1-e)}2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Writing \(L=\ell_*+s\) and \(F=t\), direct expansion gives

\[
 N_e(\ell_*+s,t)=c+d_es+h_et.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently the part of the trace integrand not contained in \(\rho\) is
the carrier

\[
 G_e(s,t)
 =\left(\frac\alpha\nu-e+s\right)
   (c+d_es+h_et)^{-r}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The parameters in (3.3) satisfy two useful identities.  If

\[
 a_e=\frac\alpha\nu-e,
\]

then

\[
 a_e=\frac{d_e+\Delta}{\nu},
 \qquad
 h_e=\frac{p-\Delta d_e-d_e^2}{2\nu^2}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The second identity is the algebraic relation that makes a branch-free
stencil possible.

For nonnegative integers \(i,j\), write

\[
 J_{ij}(e)=
 \partial_s^i\partial_t^jG_e(0,0).
\tag{3.5}
\]

## 4. The removable diagonal singularity

The historical unequal-shape four-jet identity used coefficients containing
\(1/\Delta\).  It therefore required a separate formula at
\(\Delta=0\).  The singularity is not intrinsic.  Two additional jet
channels expose a null relation that removes it.

Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_{00}&=\frac{\Delta\nu r(r+1)}p,\\
 v_{10}&=\frac{\Delta^2r(r+1)-p(r^2+r+1)}{p(r-1)},\\
 v_{20}&=-\frac{2\Delta(r+1)}{\nu(r-1)},\\
 v_{30}&=\frac{p}{\nu^2(r-1)},\\
 v_{01}&=\frac{2\Delta\nu^2r(r+1)}{p(r-1)},\\
 v_{11}&=-\frac{2\nu(r+2)}{r-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

All denominators in (4.1) are positive.  In particular, these coefficients
are finite and continuous across \(\Delta=0\).

### The six-jet identity

For every real \(e\),

\[
 \boxed{
 v_{00}J_{00}+v_{10}J_{10}+v_{20}J_{20}
 +v_{30}J_{30}+v_{01}J_{01}+v_{11}J_{11}=c^{-r}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is an identity, not an asymptotic statement.

### Proof

Put

\[
 x=\frac{d_e}{c},\qquad z=\frac{h_e}{c},\qquad a=a_e.
\]

Differentiating (3.3) gives the following complete normalized jet table:

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 (i,j)&c^rJ_{ij}\\ \hline
 (0,0)&a\\
 (1,0)&1-ra x\\
 (2,0)&-2rx+r(r+1)a x^2\\
 (3,0)&3r(r+1)x^2-r(r+1)(r+2)a x^3\\
 (0,1)&-ra z\\
 (1,1)&rz\{(r+1)ax-1\}.
\end{array}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Substitute

\[
 a=\frac{d_e+\Delta}{\nu},
 \quad x=\frac{\nu d_e}{p},
 \quad z=\frac{p-\Delta d_e-d_e^2}{2\nu p}
\tag{4.4}
\]

into (4.3), multiply the six rows by (4.1), and collect powers of
\(d_e\).  The result is

\[
 \sum_{(i,j)}v_{ij}c^rJ_{ij}-1
 =0\,d_e^4+0\,d_e^3+0\,d_e^2+0\,d_e+0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Every division used in this reduction is by one of
\(p,\nu,r-1\), all of which is nonzero.  Thus (4.5) proves (4.2) for all
positive shapes, including \(\Delta=0\).  Notice that no relation obtained
by dividing by \(\Delta\) occurs anywhere in the proof.

There is also a useful way to read the calculation.  For \(\Delta\ne0\),
the four channels \(00,10,20,01\) already solve the polynomial identity, but
their solution has a \(1/\Delta\) pole.  The six-channel system has a
one-dimensional null direction.  Choosing its \(11\)-coefficient to be

\[
 -\frac{2\nu(r+2)}{r-1}
\]

cancels every pole and forces the coefficient \(v_{30}\) in (4.1).  The
balanced formula is therefore the regular value of the unequal-shape
identity after a jet syzygy is added; it is not a separate phenomenon.

Indeed, when \(\Delta=0\),

\[
 v_{00}=v_{20}=v_{01}=0,
\]

and

\[
 v_{10}=-\frac{r^2+r+1}{r-1},\quad
 v_{30}=\frac1{4(r-1)},\quad
 v_{11}=-\frac{2\nu(r+2)}{r-1}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus (4.2) reduces directly to the balanced three-channel identity.

### Why the old four channels cannot cross the diagonal

The two added channels are not cosmetic.  At a balanced shape
\(\alpha=\beta=\gamma\), no coefficients independent of \(e\) can represent
the constant target using only the historical channels

\[
 J_{00},\quad J_{10},\quad J_{20},\quad J_{01}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Indeed, now \(\nu=2\gamma\), \(p=\gamma^2\),
\(a=d/(2\gamma)\), \(x=2d/\gamma\), and
\(z=(\gamma^2-d^2)/(4\gamma^3)\).  Suppose

\[
 A_0c^rJ_{00}+A_1c^rJ_{10}
 +A_2c^rJ_{20}+A_3c^rJ_{01}=1
\tag{4.8}
\]

for every \(d\).  In the jet table (4.3), the coefficient of \(d^2\) in
the left side of (4.8) is

\[
 -\frac{A_1r}{\gamma^2}.
\]

It must vanish, so \(A_1=0\).  But the constant coefficient of the left
side is exactly \(A_1\), and (4.8) requires it to equal one.  This is a
contradiction.  Equivalently, the four-channel coefficient matrix has rank
three while adjoining the constant target raises its rank to four.

Thus a formula confined to those four jets must either exclude the diagonal
or become singular there.  The \(30\) and \(11\) channels in (4.2) supply
the missing even-power cancellation and make the extension regular.

## 5. One eight-node finite stencil

Choose positive steps \(h,\eta\).  For a function \(G\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_{00}G&=G(0,\eta),\\
 D_{10}G&=\frac{G(h,\eta)-G(-h,\eta)}{2h},\\
 D_{20}G&=\frac{G(h,\eta)-2G(0,\eta)+G(-h,\eta)}{h^2},\\
 D_{30}G&=\frac{G(2h,\eta)-2G(h,\eta)
                  +2G(-h,\eta)-G(-2h,\eta)}{2h^3},\\
 D_{01}G&=\frac{G(0,2\eta)-G(0,\eta)}\eta,\\
 D_{11}G&=\frac{G(h,2\eta)-G(-h,2\eta)
                  -G(h,\eta)+G(-h,\eta)}{2h\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The negative finite-jet action is

\[
 A_{h,\eta}(e)=-\sum_{(i,j)}v_{ij}D_{ij}G_e.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Only the following eight nodes occur:

\[
\begin{split}
 &(-2h,\eta),(-h,\eta),(0,\eta),(h,\eta),(2h,\eta),\\
 &(-h,2\eta),(0,2\eta),(h,2\eta).
\end{split}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Writing \(A_{h,\eta}(e)=\sum_z\omega_zG_e(z)\), the exact weights are

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
z&\omega_z\\ \hline
(-2h,\eta)&v_{30}/(2h^3)\\
(-h,\eta)&v_{10}/(2h)-v_{20}/h^2-v_{30}/h^3-v_{11}/(2h\eta)\\
(0,\eta)&-v_{00}+2v_{20}/h^2+v_{01}/\eta\\
(h,\eta)&-v_{10}/(2h)-v_{20}/h^2+v_{30}/h^3+v_{11}/(2h\eta)\\
(2h,\eta)&-v_{30}/(2h^3)\\
(-h,2\eta)&v_{11}/(2h\eta)\\
(0,2\eta)&-v_{01}/\eta\\
(h,2\eta)&-v_{11}/(2h\eta).
\end{array}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Equations (5.1)--(5.4) are a single formula.  There is no equality test on
the shapes.

## 6. A uniform finite-difference margin

Set

\[
 R=\frac{\min(\alpha,\beta)}{4\nu},
 \qquad
 h_N=\frac{R}{4N},
 \qquad
 \eta_N=\frac{c}{8N},
 \qquad N\ge1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Every node in (5.3), translated by \(L=\ell_*+s\), lies in \(\Omega\).
Indeed,

\[
 2h_N\le \frac{\min(\alpha,\beta)}{8\nu}
 <\min(\ell_*,1-\ell_*),
 \qquad \eta_N>0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Moreover, for \(|s|\le R\), \(t\ge0\), and \(0\le e\le1\),

\[
 c+d_es+h_et\ge\frac{3c}{4}>0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

To see this, use

\[
 |d_e|\le\max(\alpha,\beta),
 \qquad
 |d_es|\le
 \frac{\max(\alpha,\beta)\min(\alpha,\beta)}{4\nu}
 =\frac c4,
 \qquad h_et\ge0.
\]

Thus all derivatives of \(G_e\) required by (5.1) are bounded uniformly on
the compact \(e\)-interval and the fixed local box.

For completeness, define the rising factorial by

\[
 (r)_0=1,\qquad (r)_m=r(r+1)\cdots(r+m-1)\quad(m\ge1).
\]

Put

\[
 D=\max(\alpha,\beta),\qquad H=\frac18,
 \qquad z_0=\frac{3c}{4}.
\]

For \(m=i+j\), one explicit common derivative bound is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_{ij}={}&\frac98(r)_mD^iH^jz_0^{-r-m}\\
 &+\mathbf 1_{\{i\ge1\}}
 i(r)_{m-1}D^{i-1}H^jz_0^{-r-m+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The second line is defined to be zero when \(i=0\).  To verify (6.4), use

\[
 |a_e+s|\le\frac98,
 \quad |d_e|\le\max(\alpha,\beta),
 \quad 0\le h_e\le\frac18,
 \quad c+d_es+h_et\ge\frac{3c}{4},
\tag{6.5}
\]

and the exact two-term derivative formula

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \partial_s^i\partial_t^jG_e(s,t)
 ={}&(a_e+s)(-1)^m(r)_m d_e^i h_e^j
       (c+d_es+h_et)^{-r-m}\\
 &+i(-1)^{m-1}(r)_{m-1}d_e^{i-1}h_e^j
       (c+d_es+h_et)^{-r-m+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.6}
\]

The entire second line of (6.6) is omitted when \(i=0\).

Taylor's formula applied to (5.1) then yields

\[
 \left|\sum v_{ij}D_{ij}G_e-c^{-r}\right|
 \le \frac{A_1}{N}+\frac{A_2}{N^2}
\tag{6.7}
\]

uniformly for \(0\le e\le1\), where one valid completely explicit choice is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_1={}&\frac c8\bigl(
 |v_{00}|B_{01}+|v_{10}|B_{11}+|v_{20}|B_{21}
 +|v_{30}|B_{31}+2|v_{01}|B_{02}+2|v_{11}|B_{12}
 \bigr),\\
 A_2={}&\left(\frac R4\right)^2\bigl(
 |v_{10}|B_{30}/6+|v_{20}|B_{40}/12
 +(17/60)|v_{30}|B_{50}+|v_{11}|B_{31}/6
 \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.8}
\]

The constants in (6.8) come respectively from vertical transport and the
centered first-, second-, third-, and mixed-difference remainders.  In
particular \(A_1,A_2<\infty\) for every positive shape pair.

Let

\[
 J=c^{-r}>0
\]

and choose, for example,

\[
 N=1+\left\lceil
 \max\left\{\frac{8A_1}{J},
             \sqrt{\frac{8A_2}{J}}\right\}
 \right\rceil.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Then (6.7) is strictly smaller than \(J/4\).  Combining this with (4.2)
and the minus sign in (5.2) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{0\le e\le1}A_{h_N,\eta_N}(e)
 \le-\frac{3J}{4}.}
\tag{6.10}
\]

This is already a finite atomic negative trace, uniform over the entire
compactified nuisance interval.

## 7. Replacing the atoms by product tents

For \(x\in\mathbb R\) and \(w>0\), define the normalized triangular tent

\[
 \tau_{x,w}(u)=\frac1w\left(1-\frac{|u-x|}{w}\right)_+.
\tag{7.1}
\]

It satisfies

\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}\tau_{x,w}(u)\,du=1,
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}\tau_{x,w}(u)^2\,du=\frac{2}{3w}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For a node \(z=(L_z,F_z)\), put

\[
 \Theta_{z,w}(L,F)=\tau_{L_z,w}(L)\tau_{F_z,w}(F).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Its integral is one and its squared integral is \(4/(9w^2)\).

Translate the eight local nodes (5.3) by \(L_z=\ell_*+s_z\), retain
\(F_z=t_z\), and let \(\omega_z\) be (5.4).  Since every node is in
\(\Omega\),

\[
 \rho_z:=\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L_z,F_z)>0.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Choose the preliminary width

\[
 w_0=\frac14\min\left\{
 h_N,\eta_N,\ell_*-2h_N,1-\ell_*-2h_N\right\}>0.
\tag{7.5}
\]

The eight closed product boxes of radius \(w_0\) are contained in
\(\Omega\) and are pairwise disjoint.  Also

\[
 2h_N+w_0<R,
\]

so the denominator floor \(N_e\ge z_0=3c/4\) from (6.3) holds throughout
every preliminary box, not only at its center.

For each node define on its preliminary box

\[
 H_{z,e}(L,F)
 =\frac{\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F)}{\rho_z}
   (L-e)N_e(L,F)^{-r}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

The smearing bound can also be made elementary.  For each node put

\[
 L_z^-=L_z-w_0,\quad L_z^+=L_z+w_0,
 \quad F_z^-=F_z-w_0,\quad F_z^+=F_z+w_0,
\tag{7.7}
\]

and, for \(x_->0\),

\[
 \mathsf P(a;x_-,x_+)=\max\{x_-^a,x_+^a\}.
\]

The following numbers bound \(\rho\), \(|\partial_L\rho|\), and
\(|\partial_F\rho|\), respectively, throughout the node box:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_z={}&\mathsf P(\beta-1;L_z^-,L_z^+)
 \mathsf P(\alpha-1;1-L_z^+,1-L_z^-)(F_z^+)^{1/2},\\
 R_{L,z}={}&R_z\left(
 \frac{|\beta-1|}{L_z^-}
 +\frac{|\alpha-1|}{1-L_z^+}\right),\\
 R_{F,z}={}&\frac{R_z}{2F_z^-}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Let \(z_0=3c/4\), as in (6.4), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 K_z=\frac1{\rho_z}\bigg[&R_{L,z}z_0^{-r}
 +R_z\{z_0^{-r}+rD z_0^{-r-1}\}\\
 &+R_{F,z}z_0^{-r}
 +R_z\frac r8z_0^{-r-1}\bigg],
 \qquad K=\max_zK_z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.9}
\]

Indeed, \(|L-e|\le1\), \(|\partial_LN_e|=|d_e|\le D\), and
\(0\le\partial_FN_e=h_e\le1/8\).  The product rule applied to (7.6)
therefore proves

\[
 |\partial_LH_{z,e}|+|\partial_FH_{z,e}|\le K
\tag{7.10}
\]

on every preliminary box, uniformly for \(0\le e\le1\).

Let

\[
 W=\sum_z|\omega_z|.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Equation (6.10) implies \(W>0\).  Choose canonically

\[
 w=\frac12\min\left\{w_0,
              \frac{J}{16W(1+K)}\right\}>0.
\tag{7.12}
\]

Finally define the promised profile

\[
 \boxed{
 q_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F)
 =\sum_z\frac{\omega_z}{\rho_z}\Theta_{z,w}(L,F).}
\tag{7.13}
\]

This is a bounded continuous finite sum of at most eight compactly supported
piecewise-linear product tents.  It is zero in a neighbourhood of every
boundary face.

Using (7.2), (7.6), and the fact that each product tent has integral one,

\[
 T_e(q_{\alpha,\beta})
 =\sum_z\omega_z
   \iint\Theta_{z,w}(L,F)H_{z,e}(L,F)\,dF\,dL.
\tag{7.14}
\]

The mean-value estimate, (7.10), and (7.12) give

\[
 \left|T_e(q_{\alpha,\beta})-
       \sum_z\omega_zG_e(s_z,t_z)\right|
 \le 2wKW\le\frac J8
\tag{7.15}
\]

uniformly in \(e\).  Therefore (6.10) implies

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{0\le e\le1}T_e(q_{\alpha,\beta})
 \le-\frac{5J}{8}.}
\tag{7.16}
\]

No endpoint moment is used in this argument.  Positivity and continuity of
the density are needed only on eight interior boxes.

## 8. One positive global step

Let \(S\) be the compact support of (7.13).  The bounds already used in
Section 7 give the explicit constant

\[
 K_*=\max_z R_z z_0^{-r}<\infty
\tag{8.1}
\]

such that

\[
 \rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F)N_e(L,F)^{-r}\le K_*
 \qquad(0\le e\le1,\ (L,F)\in S).
\]

The tent boxes are disjoint, so (7.2) gives the explicit uniform energy
bound

\[
 E_e(q_{\alpha,\beta})
 \le
 \frac{4K_*}{9w^2}
 \sum_z\left(\frac{\omega_z}{\rho_z}\right)^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Define

\[
 M=1+\frac{4K_*}{9w^2}
 \sum_z\left(\frac{\omega_z}{\rho_z}\right)^2,
 \qquad
 \delta=\frac{5J}{8},
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac\delta M.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Then \(M>0\), \(\varepsilon_{\alpha,\beta}>0\), and, by (7.16) and
(8.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 2\varepsilon_{\alpha,\beta}T_e(q_{\alpha,\beta})
 +\varepsilon_{\alpha,\beta}^2E_e(q_{\alpha,\beta})
 &\le-2\frac{\delta^2}{M}
      +\frac{\delta^2}{M^2}M\\
 &=-\frac{\delta^2}{M}<0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This proves (1.1).

The construction is therefore not merely sign-feasible.  It supplies one
strictly positive step that works for the entire nuisance continuum.

### Effectivity boundary

For arbitrary real \(\alpha,\beta>0\), the preceding argument is an exact
real-coefficient existence theorem.  Formula (6.9) names a finite integer,
and (7.7)--(7.12) name a positive real width through finitely many elementary
operations and maxima.  This does not turn a noncomputable real input into a
finite machine object.

For rational, algebraic, or otherwise computably presented positive shapes,
all strict inequalities in the construction have positive slack.  Rational
centers, widths, stencil weights, and density multipliers can therefore be
chosen sufficiently close to the displayed real values while preserving
(6.10), (7.16), and (8.4).  This supplies a terminating rational-output
enclosure route whenever the input reals and elementary power evaluations
are supplied effectively.  No globally rational coefficient table is
claimed for an arbitrary irrational pair supplied without such effective
data.

## 9. Exact exchange covariance

Exchange the shapes and simultaneously transform

\[
 (\alpha,\beta,e,L,F)
 \longmapsto(\beta,\alpha,1-e,1-L,F).
\tag{9.1}
\]

The quantities \(\nu,p,c,r,h_N,\eta_N,J\) are unchanged, while
\(\Delta\) changes sign and \(s=L-\ell_*\) changes to \(-s\).  Also

\[
 \rho_{\beta,\alpha}(1-L,F)=\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F),
\tag{9.2}
\]

\[
 G^{\beta,\alpha}_{1-e}(-s,t)=-G^{\alpha,\beta}_e(s,t).
\tag{9.3}
\]

In (4.1), \(v_{00},v_{20},v_{01}\) are odd in \(\Delta\), whereas
\(v_{10},v_{30},v_{11}\) are even.  It follows either from (5.1) or directly
from (5.4) that

\[
 \omega^{\beta,\alpha}_{(-s,t)}
 =-\omega^{\alpha,\beta}_{(s,t)}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

All absolute-value bounds used to select \(N\) and \(w\) are invariant under
the exchange: (6.4) is symmetric, the absolute values of odd coefficients
are unchanged, the four entries defining \(w_0\) are merely permuted, and
the finite derivative maximum \(K\) is preserved by reflection.  Choosing
the integer in (6.9) and the canonical symmetric width in (7.12),
(9.2)--(9.4) give

\[
 q_{\beta,\alpha}(1-L,F)=-q_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F),
\]

which is (1.2).  Thus the construction does not obtain the unequal-shape
case by privileging one sample.

## 10. Exact walls for uncancelled polynomial and Laurent charts

The tent construction avoids moment walls structurally.  The obstruction to
unbounded polynomial or Laurent profiles can be stated sharply.

### Radial polynomial wall

Suppose that on some closed interval \(I\subset(0,1)\), a profile has an
uncancelled polynomial leading term in \(F\):

\[
 q(L,F)=a_d(L)F^d+O(F^{d-1}),
 \qquad d\ge1,
 \qquad \inf_{L\in I}|a_d(L)|>0.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Here the \(O(F^{d-1})\) remainder is uniform for \(L\in I\).

Fix any \(0<e<1\).  Uniformly for \(L\in I\), as \(F\to\infty\),

\[
 \rho_{\alpha,\beta}(L,F)N_e(L,F)^{-r}
 \asymp F^{1/2-r}=F^{-\nu-1}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Consequently the energy tail is bounded below by a positive multiple of

\[
 \int_1^\infty F^{2d-\nu-1}\,dF.
\tag{10.3}
\]

It diverges exactly when

\[
 \nu=\alpha+\beta\le2d.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Thus no radial polynomial chart satisfying the uniform uncancelled-leading-
term hypothesis (10.1) has finite quadratic energy for all positive Gamma
shapes.  This does not exclude a separately designed chart in which the
purported leading coefficient cancels identically.

### Boundary Laurent walls

More precisely, fix a compact interval \(I_F\subset(0,\infty)\).  Suppose

\[
 q(L,F)=a(F)L^{-k}+o(L^{-k})\quad(L\downarrow0)
\tag{10.5}
\]

uniformly for \(F\in I_F\), where \(k>0\) and
\(\inf_{F\in I_F}|a(F)|>0\).  Then the energy contains a positive multiple
of

\[
 \int_0 L^{\beta-1-2k}\,dL,
\tag{10.6}
\]

which diverges when \(\beta\le2k\).  A term of order \((1-L)^{-k}\) similarly
diverges when \(\alpha\le2k\), under the reflected uniform leading-term
hypothesis.

These are not defects of a particular coefficient search.  They are exact
integrability obstructions for charts carrying the stated uncancelled terms.
Any architecture intended to cover the whole positive shape quadrant must
either cancel all such leading terms or avoid the walls.  The profile (7.13)
takes the latter route: it is bounded and has
compact interior support.

## 11. Scope of the construction

The construction resolves the explicit-profile question at the following
scope.

- Every asymmetric positive shape pair is covered.
- Every equal positive shape pair is covered by the same formula.
- Shapes below every historical inverse-moment threshold are covered.
- Irrational positive shapes are covered at the reduced Gamma level.
- The profile has at most eight product tents, a finite explicit coefficient
  table, a finite shape-adaptive resolution prescription, a positive common
  nuisance-uniform step, and exact exchange covariance.  The prescription is
  a terminating certified procedure when the shapes are computably
  presented.

The scope has the following distinctions.

- There is no shape-independent numerical table of centers, widths, or
  coefficients.
- There is no positive risk margin asserted uniformly as
  \(\alpha\downarrow0\), \(\beta\downarrow0\), or the shapes escape to
  infinity.
- Eight tents are not claimed to be minimal.
- The corrected rule is not claimed terminal or admissible against every
  measurable competitor.
- The theorem does not close any all-Borel terminality or certificate-ladder
  endpoint beyond this explicit strict-profile problem.

The essential mathematical closure is the diagonal-regular identity (4.2).
Once that identity is available, finite differences turn its positive
constant target into a uniform negative atomic trace; interior tent smearing
preserves the trace; compact support controls energy; and one quadratic step
finishes the strict inequality.  The moment walls are bypassed rather than
crossed by an illicit inverse moment.
